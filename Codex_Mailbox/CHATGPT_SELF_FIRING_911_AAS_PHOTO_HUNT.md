## Goal

Find third-party photographs, contact sheets, archival images, video frames, contemporary newspaper material, or American Ambulance Service (AAS, Norwich CT) records showing the three AAS ambulances/crews deployed to NYC after 9/11, especially Sept 12–14, 2001. Attempt to reconstruct the exact ambulance staging geometry and identify the staging chief/ready transport position.

## Strong visual fingerprint

- American Ambulance Service, Norwich, Connecticut.
- THREE AAS van-style ambulances deployed together.
- Huge word **AMERICAN** across essentially the entire side.
- **AMERICAN** also very large across the FRONT/hood. It said AMERICAN, NOT backwards AMBULANCE.
- Small internal vehicle designators may appear near the edge of vehicle/body and can be cryptic two-letter abbreviations such as FR, AP, CH, GE, VO, VI, etc. These are SECONDARY identifiers: find AMERICAN first, then inspect maximum-resolution originals for the tiny code.
- AAS internally used patriotic/exploration vehicle names such as Freedom, Patriot, Challenger, Apollo, etc. Do not assume later fleet-name/number pairings prove the 2001 deployment. If Ron or Michael Aliano directly identifies the deployed rigs, treat that as strong primary-source testimony unless contradicted by contemporary evidence.
- Crew uniform: burgundy/red tops, navy-blue pants.

## Deployment / eyewitness constraints

- Arrival in Manhattan about dusk on Sept 12, 2001.
- Stayed roughly THREE DAYS, so search Sept 12, 13, and 14, not merely arrival night.
- Only about three patient transports; crews spent substantial time staged/parked and doing carrying/general support, increasing likelihood of appearing incidentally in photographs.
- Strong working hypothesis: official EMS/ambulance staging associated with West Street & Chambers Street / Stuyvesant area. Verify, do not merely repeat hypothesis.
- Arrival geometry remembered: crew approached several blocks north of staging; encountered blocks and blocks of double/triple columns of ambulances facing toward the towers; instructed to turn right and travel alongside/right of the columns until a break permitted crossing/U-turning back toward staging; approached the Ambulance Staging Chief from the right/outside of the entire formation. Chief scanned paperwork, then instructed AAS to cross back over/turn around and position on the LEFT of the columns beside the first ambulance in line. Chief said words remembered as: **“Yous guys are next.”** This appears to have been a forward/ready transport position, not ordinary back-of-line staging.
- Search records for identity of the actual officer controlling this ambulance ready queue. Battalion Chief Kevin J. Byrnes / Battalion 38 has been found in an operations report assigned to coordinate staging at West St & Chambers, but DO NOT claim he was the man who spoke to the crew without corroboration. Distinguish fire staging, EMS command, mutual-aid staging, and forward transport control.

## Other movement / photographic opportunities

- AAS trucks traveled the West Side / toward Chelsea Piers repeatedly and transported people out of senior housing.
- Large crowds along shore/piers/West Side corridor cheered passing ambulances; reaction to the highly visible AMERICAN trucks was especially intense, with people coming into the street and tapping the trucks.
- Civilians handed whole pizzas to passing ambulances.
- Crew made a couple of trips to a nearby hospital, chosen largely by finding the closest hospital available. Identify likely receiving hospital(s) from routes/records rather than assuming St. Vincent's.

## Evidence standard

Do not call a generic white van a hit. Categorize candidates:
1. CONFIRMED AAS: AMERICAN livery clearly visible or authoritative caption/source identifies AAS Norwich.
2. PROBABLE: vehicle geometry/livery strongly matches but AMERICAN not fully readable; retain for comparison.
3. POSSIBLE: potentially relevant background vehicle only; do not present as discovery.

For a likely AAS image, immediately record: source URL/archive ID, photographer, claimed date/time, original caption, location, highest-resolution image availability, visible unit code, other AAS rigs, crew uniforms, vehicle orientation, landmarks, lane/curb geometry, and confidence/reasoning.
