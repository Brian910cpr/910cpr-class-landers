CPR Training for Churches and Ministry Teams

Role-based on-site CPR, AED, First Aid, and related training guidance for churches, ministries, and faith organizations, with a simple path to request training.

Program summaryChoose adult-only, adult-and-pediatric, or broader emergency readiness based on programs and attendees. Volunteers can practice the same response plan even when experience levels differ.

Emergencies to prepare for

Cardiac arrest, choking, injuries, and emergencies involving children can occur during services, nursery care, and large events. AED readiness and a coordinated response matter.

Who should be trained

Consider ushers, security teams, childcare volunteers, clergy, and ministry leaders. Match each person to the role and written requirement rather than placing everyone in one course.

Useful scenarios and supplemental practice

Practice a collapse during worship, a choking emergency at a meal, an injury during an event, and an infant or child emergency in the nursery.

Equipment and response planning

AED access should account for locked buildings, multiple floors, large gatherings, and volunteers who may not know the property. Ushers and security teams can rehearse directing EMS.

Scheduling, new hires, and renewals

Volunteer schedules, nursery coverage, weekend events, and mixed experience levels often favor staged groups with the same written response plan. Track new volunteers separately from expiring cardholders.

What 910CPR needs to arrange training

Share the complete training address, group size, employee roles, exact employer or licensing requirement, learning preference, workday constraints, and preferred dates. Choose a preferred time, and 910CPR will confirm travel, instructor availability, and final pricing before the class is scheduled.

Certification and added practice

A certification card applies only to its completed certification course and certifying organization. Bloodborne Pathogens, Bleeding Control, choking practice, and organization scenarios may be separate additions. American Heart Association, American Red Cross, and Health & Safety Institute credentials are not interchangeable when a requirement names one.

Common questions

Who should be on a church response team?

A practical team may include ushers, security, clergy, ministry leaders, nursery volunteers, and staff who are commonly present during gatherings.

Can training happen at our facility?

Yes, when the address, room, group size, equipment plan, travel, and instructor schedule are confirmed.

What does our coordinator need to provide?

Employee roles, exact requirement wording, headcount, address, workday constraints, and preferred dates are enough to begin.
