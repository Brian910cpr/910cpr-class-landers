CPR and First Aid Training for Restaurants and Waitstaff

Role-based on-site CPR, AED, First Aid, and related training guidance for restaurants and food-service teams, with a simple path to request training.

Program summaryFirst Aid/CPR/AED is usually a better starting point than BLS for ordinary restaurant roles. Training can be planned before opening or around shifts.

Emergencies to prepare for

Adult CPR and AED, choking response, burns, cuts, falls, sudden illness, and bleeding control are practical priorities. Bloodborne Pathogens awareness may be relevant where occupational exposure can occur.

Who should be trained

Consider waitstaff, kitchen staff, supervisors, and managers. Match each person to the role and written requirement rather than placing everyone in one course.

Useful scenarios and supplemental practice

Practice a guest choking response from recognition through handoff, a kitchen burn or cut, a fall in the dining room, and severe bleeding until EMS arrives.

Equipment and response planning

AED placement should be visible to staff and reachable without crossing a locked office. Managers should know who calls 911, who meets responders, and who keeps the scene clear.

Scheduling, new hires, and renewals

Short sessions before opening, between meal periods, or across two shift groups can protect service coverage. New-hire turnover makes a repeatable onboarding and renewal list especially useful.

What 910CPR needs to arrange training

Share the complete training address, group size, employee roles, exact employer or licensing requirement, learning preference, workday constraints, and preferred dates. Choose a preferred time, and 910CPR will confirm travel, instructor availability, and final pricing before the class is scheduled.

Certification and added practice

A certification card applies only to its completed certification course and certifying organization. Bloodborne Pathogens, Bleeding Control, choking practice, and organization scenarios may be separate additions. American Heart Association, American Red Cross, and Health & Safety Institute credentials are not interchangeable when a requirement names one.

Common questions

Does a restaurant team need BLS?

Usually not. BLS is intended for healthcare and professional-rescuer roles unless an employer or contract specifically requires it.

Can training happen at our facility?

Yes, when the address, room, group size, equipment plan, travel, and instructor schedule are confirmed.

What does our coordinator need to provide?

Employee roles, exact requirement wording, headcount, address, workday constraints, and preferred dates are enough to begin.
