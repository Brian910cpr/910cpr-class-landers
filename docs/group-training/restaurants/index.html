Restaurants and food service

Restaurant Choking Response, CPR & First Aid

If a guest chokes or a coworker is burned, your team needs to know who acts and who calls for help. Train kitchen and dining-room staff around a break in service.

Course family

Workplace First Aid, CPR & AED

Course type

  • Fully in person is our usual recommendation. Your team can ask questions, practice together and work through scenarios as a group. It’s often the more economical choice.
  • Blended, where allowed: Employees complete the online coursework on their own schedule, then rotate through the required instructor-led, hands-on skills in pairs or groups of four.

We’re happy to quote either format or both so you can compare, as long as the program, employer and applicable authority allow them.

Who to train

Include shift leads and first responders from the dining room and kitchen. Choose a time that keeps service and emergency coverage running.

Training fit

Which class should we consider?

Ask for First Aid, CPR and AED with a focus on choking and common kitchen injuries. Let us know if children regularly dine with you.

Plan a session

Plan around a service break and identify who calls EMS and directs responders to the correct entrance.

Tell us how many people need training and where you’d like to meet. We’ll help find a format and date that work for your team.

Primary sources

What do the authorities say?

When the general-industry rule applies and there’s no nearby medical facility used for injured employees, your workplace needs trained first-aid help. A food-handler card covers a different requirement.

General workplace rule, not a food-handler credential: 29 CFR 1910.151(b), where applicable

Plain-English summary above.
