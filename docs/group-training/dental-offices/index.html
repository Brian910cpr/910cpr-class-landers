BLS and Bloodborne Pathogens Training for Dental Offices

Role-based on-site CPR, AED, First Aid, and related training guidance for dental practices, with a direct path to legitimate scheduling.

Emergencies this team should be ready for

Clinical teams may face medical emergencies and occupational exposure. BLS, AED and bag-mask skills where appropriate, Bloodborne Pathogens, and practice-specific scenarios can be coordinated.

Who should be trained

Consider dentists, hygienists, assistants, and front-office personnel. Match each person to the role and written requirement rather than placing everyone in one course.

Which certification is usually appropriate?

Clinical staff often need BLS, while front-office staff may need First Aid/CPR/AED. A mixed-role program avoids placing every employee into BLS automatically.

Useful scenarios and supplemental practice

Practice syncope, cardiac or respiratory deterioration, team CPR around the dental chair, AED use, and a coordinated bag-mask response when the selected course and roles support it.

Equipment and response planning

Review AED access, barrier devices, emergency oxygen or other practice equipment, exposure-control procedures, and who directs front-office staff during a clinical event.

Scheduling, new hires, and renewals

Patient schedules make a whole-practice closure expensive. Clinical and front-office groups can be planned separately, while one coordinator tracks BLS, First Aid/CPR/AED, and Bloodborne Pathogens needs.

What 910CPR needs to arrange training

Share the complete training address, group size, employee roles, exact employer or licensing requirement, learning preference, workday constraints, and preferred dates. Choose a preferred time, and 910CPR will confirm travel, instructor availability, and final pricing before the class is scheduled.

Certification and added practice

A certification card applies only to its completed certification course and certifying organization. Bloodborne Pathogens, Bleeding Control, choking practice, and organization scenarios may be separate additions. American Heart Association, American Red Cross, and Health & Safety Institute credentials are not interchangeable when a requirement names one.

Common questions

Does every dental employee need BLS?

No. Dentists, hygienists, assistants, and other clinical staff may need BLS; front-office roles may have a different requirement. Use the practice’s exact governing wording.

Can training happen at our facility?

Yes, when the address, room, group size, equipment plan, travel, and instructor schedule are confirmed.

What does our coordinator need to provide?

Employee roles, exact requirement wording, headcount, address, workday constraints, and preferred dates are enough to begin.
