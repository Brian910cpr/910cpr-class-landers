CPR and AED Training for Offices and Workplaces

Role-based on-site CPR, AED, First Aid, and related training guidance for offices, worksites, and employee response teams, with a direct path to legitimate scheduling.

Emergencies this team should be ready for

Sudden cardiac arrest, falls, bleeding, burns, and acute illness are foreseeable across many workplaces. AED placement and an internal response plan support the trained team.

Who should be trained

Consider designated responders, supervisors, safety staff, and interested employees. Match each person to the role and written requirement rather than placing everyone in one course.

Which certification is usually appropriate?

An OSHA-accepted First Aid/CPR/AED program is commonly appropriate, subject to the employer’s exact requirement. BLS is not the default for general employees.

Useful scenarios and supplemental practice

Practice sudden cardiac arrest, a fall, severe bleeding, burns or chemical exposure where relevant, and the exact internal alarm and EMS handoff process.

Equipment and response planning

Designated responders should know AED and first-aid-kit locations, access restrictions, PPE, communication methods, and how site hazards affect scene safety.

Scheduling, new hires, and renewals

Multiple shifts, production coverage, contractor rules, and site orientation can require staggered groups. Keep alternates trained and add new responders as staffing changes.

What 910CPR needs to arrange training

Share the complete training address, group size, employee roles, exact employer or licensing requirement, learning preference, workday constraints, and preferred dates. Choose a preferred time, and 910CPR will confirm travel, instructor availability, and final pricing before the class is scheduled.

Certification and added practice

A certification card applies only to its completed certification course and certifying organization. Bloodborne Pathogens, Bleeding Control, choking practice, and organization scenarios may be separate additions. American Heart Association, American Red Cross, and Health & Safety Institute credentials are not interchangeable when a requirement names one.

Common questions

Is BLS the right workplace course?

Not for most general employee response teams. First Aid/CPR/AED is the usual starting point unless the employer or role specifically requires BLS.

Can training happen at our facility?

Yes, when the address, room, group size, equipment plan, travel, and instructor schedule are confirmed.

What does our coordinator need to provide?

Employee roles, exact requirement wording, headcount, address, workday constraints, and preferred dates are enough to begin.
