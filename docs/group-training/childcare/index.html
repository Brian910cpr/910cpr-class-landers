Course family

CPR, AED & pediatric / general first aid

Course type

  • Fully in person is our usual recommendation. Your team can ask questions, practice together and work through scenarios as a group. It’s often the more economical choice.
  • Blended, where allowed: Employees complete the online coursework on their own schedule, then rotate through the required instructor-led, hands-on skills in pairs or groups of four.

For licensed NC child care centers, we verify the exact approved course, any delivery restrictions and instructor evaluation before quoting a blended option. A self-paced online-only course is not a substitute.

We’re happy to quote either format or both so you can compare, as long as the program, employer and applicable authority allow them.

Who to train

Include direct-care staff and adults who accompany children offsite. Tell us whether your team works with infants, toddlers or older children.

Primary sources

What do the authorities say?

North Carolina requires age-appropriate first aid and CPR for center staff who provide direct care or accompany children offsite, plus current first-aid and CPR coverage while children are in care. DCDEE lists HSI Pediatric First Aid/CPR/AED for relevant child and infant ages; listed HSI Pediatric CPR AED or BLS cover CPR only. Check delivery restrictions and any age modules on the card.

North Carolina rule and specific course acceptance: 10A NCAC 09 .1102(c)–(d); NC DCDEE’s July 2025 HSI course list

Short official excerpt

“CPR training shall be renewed on or before the expiration of the certification.”

If you have a written requirement, send it with your request. We’ll check the course and format with you before booking.

Common question

Can I use an online-only course?

Do not assume it qualifies. NC center rules restrict distance learning for First Aid, and the approved-program listing distinguishes course formats.
