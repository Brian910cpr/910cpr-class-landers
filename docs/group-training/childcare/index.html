Pediatric First Aid and CPR for Childcare Centers

Role-based on-site CPR, AED, First Aid, and related training guidance for childcare centers and early education programs, with a direct path to legitimate scheduling.

Emergencies this team should be ready for

Pediatric breathing, cardiac, choking, allergic, illness, and injury emergencies require age-appropriate practice and a coordinated facility response.

Who should be trained

Consider teachers, directors, float staff, and other caregivers. Match each person to the role and written requirement rather than placing everyone in one course.

Which certification is usually appropriate?

Pediatric First Aid/CPR/AED is a common starting point, subject to exact licensing and employer wording. Confirm the card and agency named in that requirement.

Useful scenarios and supplemental practice

Practice infant and child CPR, choking, severe allergic reaction, asthma, seizures, injuries, burns, and the handoff from classroom staff to the director and EMS.

Equipment and response planning

Plan access to age-appropriate first-aid supplies and an AED when provided. Confirm how staff summon help while maintaining supervision and accounting for every child.

Scheduling, new hires, and renewals

Ratios, nap schedules, pickup periods, licensing wording, and staff turnover shape delivery. Directors often need a plan for onboarding new staff between whole-team renewal cycles.

What 910CPR needs to arrange training

Share the complete training address, group size, employee roles, exact employer or licensing requirement, learning preference, workday constraints, and preferred dates. Choose a preferred time, and 910CPR will confirm travel, instructor availability, and final pricing before the class is scheduled.

Certification and added practice

A certification card applies only to its completed certification course and certifying organization. Bloodborne Pathogens, Bleeding Control, choking practice, and organization scenarios may be separate additions. American Heart Association, American Red Cross, and Health & Safety Institute credentials are not interchangeable when a requirement names one.

Common questions

Which pediatric course should a childcare center choose?

Start with the exact licensing or employer wording. Pediatric First Aid/CPR/AED is a common fit, but the required certifying organization and delivery format must be confirmed.

Can training happen at our facility?

Yes, when the address, room, group size, equipment plan, travel, and instructor schedule are confirmed.

What does our coordinator need to provide?

Employee roles, exact requirement wording, headcount, address, workday constraints, and preferred dates are enough to begin.
